## Lab objective
Evaluate three recommenders on a small movie-ratings dataset (12 users, 20 movies, 120 ratings) using rating-prediction metrics (MAE, RMSE), Top-3 ranking metrics (Precision@K, Recall@K, NDCG@K) and beyond-accuracy metrics (catalogue coverage, novelty, diversity), then recommend a model for a stated business objective. Train/test split: each user's most recent rating is held out (108 train / 12 test).

## Models compared
1. Popularity baseline (movie mean rating; ranking by mean rating then rating count)
2. Content-based filtering (genre features, rating-weighted user profile, cosine similarity)
3. Item-based collaborative filtering (item-item cosine similarity, similarity-weighted average of the user's ratings)

## Results
| Model | MAE | RMSE | Precision@3 | Recall@3 | NDCG@3 | Coverage | Novelty | Diversity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Popularity Baseline | 1.086 | 1.201 | 0.067 | 0.20 | 0.10 | 0.35 | 1.689 | 0.736 |
| Content-Based | 1.094 | 1.381 | 0.067 | 0.20 | 0.20 | 0.70 | 1.344 | 0.542 |
| Item-Based CF | 0.924 | 1.070 | 0.000 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.85 | 1.695 | 0.780 |

## Findings
- **Best model for rating prediction:** Item-Based CF (lowest MAE and RMSE). Content-Based did not beat the popularity baseline.
- **Best model for ranking:** Content-Based (highest NDCG@3). Item-Based CF had no Top-3 hits, so lower rating error did not give better ranking.
- **Coverage, novelty, diversity:** Popularity covers only 35% of the catalogue and recommends nearly the same titles to everyone. CF has the highest coverage (85%) and diversity (0.78) with novelty similar to popularity. Content-Based has 70% coverage but the lowest novelty (1.34) and diversity (0.54).
- **Extension (Option A, K = 1, 3, 5):** Recall@K rises with K while Precision@K falls. Content-Based leads at K = 1 and K = 5.

## Final recommended model
**Content-Based filtering** for a service that wants relevant discovery without popular titles dominating: it is the only model that beat the baseline on ranking and it doubles catalogue coverage versus popularity. The accepted trade-off is lower novelty and diversity. A hybrid (content + CF with diversity re-ranking) is the natural next step.

## Limitations
1. Tiny test set: one held-out rating per user (12 total) and only 5 relevant items, so one user changes ranking metrics by up to 20 percentage points; results are indicative, not statistically reliable.
2. A single leave-last-out split with no repeated runs or confidence intervals.
3. Content features are genre only, and the user profile mixes how often a genre is watched with how much it is liked.
4. Only 20 movies and 10 ratings per user, so item-item similarities are sparse and noisy (zero-filled).
5. Relevance (rating >= 4) is a minority class, and novelty/diversity definitions are simple teaching versions.
